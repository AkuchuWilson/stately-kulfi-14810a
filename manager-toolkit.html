Tool 4

Scenario practice cards

Training scenarios, not real customer stories. Cut along the dashed lines. Use one card per pair.

Training scenario 1 of 7

A customer who is unsure what they need

“I don’t really know what I need. Just show me something good.”

Try: Offer an easy way in: ask what they will mostly use the shoes for and how their current pair feels.

Avoid: Bringing the bestseller before you understand the need.

Discuss: Why does this response help or harm the customer?

Training scenario 2 of 7

A customer with a limited budget

Their budget is $120. The closest match is $165.

Try: Show a suitable option within budget and the closer match, explain the difference honestly, let them try both.

Avoid: Showing only the dearer pair, or saying nothing suitable exists.

Discuss: Why does this response help or harm the customer?

Training scenario 3 of 7

A customer who disagrees with a scan explanation

“That’s wrong. My feet are fine.”

Try: Respect their view, explain the scan only shows how they stand today, and let fit and feel decide.

Avoid: Arguing, repeating the findings, or naming a condition.

Discuss: Why does this response help or harm the customer?

Training scenario 4 of 7

A suitable product is out of stock

The best match is not available in their size.

Try: Be honest, offer to order or hold it, and show one suitable alternative to try and compare.

Avoid: Fitting the wrong size or presenting the alternative as your first choice.

Discuss: Why does this response help or harm the customer?

Training scenario 5 of 7

An additional product would not help

The customer is happy with a well-fitting shoe; an insole would not add meaningful benefit.

Try: Do not recommend it. Mention it only if their needs change.

Avoid: Adding it because it raises the sale.

Discuss: Why does this response help or harm the customer?

Training scenario 6 of 7

An unhappy customer returning footwear

“You told me these would fix my pain.”

Try: Listen, acknowledge, check the fit, explain options under store policy, and suggest a podiatrist if pain continues.

Avoid: Blaming the customer or promising another product will fix the pain.

Discuss: Why does this response help or harm the customer?

Training scenario 7 of 7

A busy store with several customers waiting

Three people are waiting while you finish a fitting.

Try: Acknowledge the waiting customers briefly, give your customer a ten-second summary, and confirm before moving on.

Avoid: Walking away without a word, or skipping the summary.

Discuss: Why does this response help or harm the customer?

Tool 6

Practical assessment and assessor guide

Used for the Certified Fit Specialist practical assessment and for internal sign-off. The online exam (30 questions, 24 to pass) is separate and stays in place.

Two scenarios, both required

  • Scenario A, standard fitting: a customer on their feet all day; assess, explain, recommend and validate.
  • Scenario B, difficult interaction: the customer disagrees with the scan, has a limited budget, and an extra product would not help them.

Pass criteria

  • Every criterion rated at least Demonstrated with support
  • At least four of six criteria Demonstrated independently
  • No critical errors in either scenario

Critical errors mean correction and reassessment: an unsupported diagnosis or medical claim, an unsafe recommendation, or pressure after the customer declines. Give feedback the same day, agree one coaching action, and reassess with a different scenario after at least seven days of practice.

Scoring examples: clear explanation

  • Independently: “This shows more pressure at the front, which fits the aching you mentioned.”
  • With support: correct, but needed a prompt to link it to the customer.
  • Not yet: reads out numbers or uses medical terms.

Scoring examples: customer choice

  • Independently: customer walks in two options and chooses.
  • With support: compared options after a prompt.
  • Not yet: staff member decides for the customer.

Time, kept separate

  • Online learning: lessons and knowledge checks
  • Workplace practice: role-plays, huddles and shifts
  • Assessment: about 30 minutes for both scenarios and feedback
