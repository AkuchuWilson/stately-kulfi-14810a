Do you need a foot scanner to fit shoes well?

3 minute read · BrightPath Academy

Foot scanners can measure length, width, arch height and pressure in seconds, and customers often find the results engaging. But many excellent fittings happen in stores and clinics without a scanner.

Route 1: scanner-supported fitting

  • Ask permission before scanning.
  • Explain what the measurements can and cannot show.
  • Focus on the findings that relate to the customer's needs.
  • Ask whether the result matches how their feet feel.

Route 2: hands-on fitting

  • Ask where current footwear feels tight, loose or uncomfortable.
  • Observe current shoes and how the customer stands and walks.
  • Measure length and width of both feet.
  • Compare shoe shape, width, depth and volume.

One customer standard

Both routes finish the same way: the customer tries the options, compares them and chooses. Technology adds evidence; the specialist connects it to what the customer says and feels. Neither route replaces clinical care, and anything that looks painful or unusual should be referred.

General information for footwear and front-of-house teams. It is not medical advice; anyone with foot pain or a health concern should see a qualified clinician.
